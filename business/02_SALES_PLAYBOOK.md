# Sales Playbook

You need **7 clients**. Not 700. That reframes everything below: this is a
high-touch, low-volume sale where every conversation matters.

---

## The core insight

Do not sell "AI SEO". Nobody wakes up wanting AI SEO.

Sell **a specific, measured fact about their business that they did not know
and will not like**:

> *"Apex Heating & Air appears in 0 of 4 AI answers for HVAC in Austin.
> Summit Climate Co appears in all 4."*

The fleet produces that line automatically for every prospect, before you ever
speak to them, at a cost of fractions of a cent. That is the entire pitch. The
rest of this document is just how to not fumble it.

## The qualification bar

Only pitch a business where **all four** are true. The `outreach` agent enforces
the first three in code:

1. Visibility score under 55 (there is a real problem).
2. A competitor is measurably beating them (there is a real threat).
3. They have a website (there is something to fix).
4. They spend money on marketing already — trucks with wraps, a Google Ads
   presence, a real GBP listing. *(Check this manually before a call.)*

A business with no website and no marketing spend is not a cheap client. It is
a client who will not pay, will not implement, and will churn. Skip them.

## Email sequence

The fleet drafts all three. Review before approving.

**Email 1 — the evidence.** Subject: *"[Business] isn't showing up in AI search
for [City]"*. Names the gap, names the competitor, offers the full report free.
No attachment, no images, no links beyond the unsubscribe.

**Email 2 (day 4) — the reframe.** The point most owners miss: *this is not a
Google ranking problem*. A business can sit at #1 in local search and still be
absent from the AI answer, because assistants build answers from structured data
and corroborating sources, not from the results page. This email closes more than
the first one does, because it explains why their existing SEO spend didn't stop this.

**Email 3 (day 8) — the close-out.** Permission to say no. "If this isn't a
priority, that's fine and I'll leave you alone." Reliably produces a chunk of
the total replies.

Then stop. Three touches, then suppressed. A fourth touch converts nobody and
costs complaint rate — which is the one resource that, once spent, cannot be
bought back.

## The cold call (2 minutes)

The phone console's **Call list** (Pipeline tab) shows who to ring, best
times first, in *their* time zone. **Start calling** runs a session: each
business opens with its number, the one true thing the audit found, the
script and the voicemail; tap what happened and the next one opens. The
phone dials; nothing in the system does.

- **Call back later**: put in the time they said. They come back to the top
  of the list then, and not before.
- **Booked a call**: put in the time agreed. You get an *Add to my calendar*
  button, and a confirmation with their report is drafted for them. On the
  day, Up next puts the walkthrough first, with their full report a tap away.

The only goal of a cold call is **their email address for the free report**,
or a booked 15-minute walkthrough. Not a sale.

1. **Open with the finding.** "Hi, is this the owner? This is [name] with
   [brand]. I'll be quick. When I asked ChatGPT 'best plumber in Waco for an
   emergency', it recommended [competitor]. You didn't come up. Did you know
   that was happening?" Then stop talking.
2. **If they're listening:** more people ask AI for a recommendation than
   scroll the results; it names two or three businesses; you've put together a
   free report on why they're missing and the three fixes.
3. **Ask:** "Can I email it over? What's the best address?" Type it in and
   tap **Send them the report**. The report is drafted with "Thanks for taking
   my call" at the top; approve it in the inbox the same day.
4. **Voicemail:** about 20 seconds, on screen. Say your number twice.

**Best times:** 8–9:30am and 4–6pm their time, weekdays. Owners are on jobs
in between. The list puts those first.

**The rules**, because calling businesses is only exempt from the Do Not Call
rules when you keep them:

- Dial by hand, to the number the business publishes.
- Only say what the report shows. Never suggest you're with Google, OpenAI or
  any AI company, and never promise a ranking.
- **"Not interested" ends it.** Say thanks, hang up, tap **Not interested**:
  no more calls or emails to them. "Don't call" is the same, harder.

Four attempts at most, a day or two apart. The list stops showing a business
after that, or after any real conversation.

## The call (20 minutes)

Do not present. **Show them the report and stay quiet.**

1. **Minutes 0–2.** "Before I walk through this — when someone needs a
   [service] and doesn't already know you, how do you think they find you today?"
   Let them answer. They will say Google, or referrals. That answer is the
   opening.
2. **Minutes 2–8.** Screen-share the report. Walk the test log — *the actual
   questions, the actual answers*. Stop at the competitor row. Then say nothing.
   The silence does the work.
3. **Minutes 8–12.** "Three things are causing this." Schema, answer-shaped
   content, GBP and citations. Keep it concrete, skip the jargon.
4. **Minutes 12–15.** The offer. "I do this monthly. $997. You get the report,
   the schema, the content, and the action plan. Most of it I hand you ready to
   paste; if you'd rather I implement it, that's the $1,997 tier."
5. **Minutes 15–20.** Objections, then ask for the card. Do not offer to
   "send over a proposal" — that is where these deals go to die. Take payment
   on the call or set a specific follow-up time. On a yes, tap **Walkthrough
   done?** on their call sheet, then **Sign them up**: the payment link is in
   their inbox before you hang up. Up next asks how it went half an hour
   after the start time if you forget.

## Objection handling

**"How do I know this works?"**
> You'll see the score in your first report and again 30 days later. If it hasn't
> moved and you've implemented what we sent, I'll refund the month. That's the
> whole guarantee — I can't promise a ranking, and anyone who does is lying.

**"Can't I just do this myself?"**
> Yes, honestly. The schema is public spec and the checklist is on your GBP
> dashboard. What you're paying for is that it happens every month without you
> thinking about it, and that someone is watching the number. Most owners who
> try it get through month one and stop.

**"$997 is a lot."**
> What's one [AC replacement / implant case / signed client] worth to you?
> *(HVAC: $6–12k. Dental implant: $3–5k. Legal: $5k+.)* If this brings one extra
> in a year it's paid for twice. And you're not locked in — cancel any time.

**"I already pay an SEO guy."**
> Good, keep them. They're working on where you rank on the results page. This
> is about whether you get named in the answer *above* the results page. Ask
> them what they're doing about AI Overviews — if they have a real answer, you
> don't need me.

**"Let me think about it."**
> Of course. What specifically do you want to think about?
> *(The real objection is behind this. Answer that one.)*

## Pricing rules

- **Lead with Growth ($997).** Starter exists to catch the price-sensitive, not
  to be the default. Never open with it.
- **Never discount the price. Add value instead** — an extra month, an extra
  location, implementation help. A discounted client churns fastest and
  negotiates every renewal.
- **The $297 audit is the fallback close**, not an opener. "Let's just do the
  audit and see what it says" recovers a call that is not closing.
- **Annual prepay at 10× monthly** (two months free). Solves cash flow and
  eliminates churn for a year. Offer it to anyone who hesitates on commitment.

| Day | Activity | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Review and approve the week's outreach batch | 30 min |
| Tue–Thu | Take calls; approve daily batches | 60 min/day |
| Fri | Read the "Your week" email; change the one thing it names | 10 min |

Roughly **5 hours a week on sales**. Everything else runs itself.

## The numbers that matter

Track these weekly. If the funnel is broken, it is broken at exactly one of
these steps and the numbers will tell you which:

| Metric | Healthy | If below |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emails delivered | >95% | Deliverability problem — stop sending, fix DNS |
| Reply rate | 1–3% | Offer or targeting problem — rewrite the subject line first |
| Reply → call | >40% | The report isn't compelling — add competitor detail |
| Call → close | >30% | Presentation problem — talk less, show the test log |
| Month-2 retention | >90% | Delivery problem — the client isn't seeing value |
